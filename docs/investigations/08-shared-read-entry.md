# Investigation 08 — Shared spell-tome read entry

## Result and scope

On 14 November 2026 the opt-in entry probe installed on Skyrim 1.7.104 and observed the user's placed-world READ with Read Or Take enabled and an inventory READ. It forwards reads and does not deny learning or mutate inventory. The tested TAKE produced no read entry. The observations are specific to this load order and disposable save; they do not establish universal coverage, safe hook chaining, or protection semantics.

## Target and implementation evidence

CommonLib declares `TESObjectBOOK::Read(TESObjectREFR*)` returning `bool` and resolves it with `RELOCATION_ID(17439, 17842)`. On the tested executable the AE relocation resolves to image RVA `0x280290`. The on-disk entry starts `48 8B C4 56 57 41 55 41 56 41 57 48 83 EC 50`; the existing 0x249-byte FNV-1a body check is `A0B7B66E2B2D53F0`. The runtime guard required both the relocation and this hash, as well as the original five bytes at all three old call sites. A successful log entry indicates those live checks passed before the detour. The observed player/book/spell IDs and returned true on learning, followed by an outbound event, support the ABI on the tested paths; an address and a matching prologue alone do not prove caller coverage or all return semantics.

The opt-in file `UntamedTomes.TraceReadEntry` selects a SafetyHook v0.6.9 function-entry detour. `UntamedTomes.TraceRead` selects the existing three call-site patches. Both markers present refuse installation; the two installers are in opposite branches. Neither marker means neither probe. The entry is restricted to runtime 1.7.104 and rejects the four named competing modules and changed target or call-site bytes. SafetyHook relocates displaced instructions into its trampoline; the thunk invokes that trampoline once in its source and returns the result. The `original_invoked=1` exit field is emitted after the forwarding call returns, not an independent machine-level call counter. The hook does not intentionally chain an existing entry patch: the body hash and named-module checks refuse known and pre-existing changes. They cannot detect later patches or all competitors, and chaining was not tested. A narrow installation-time race and exception safety deserve review before broader use; the detour remains experimental.

The prior DLL/PDB are preserved as `task08e-preservation/schema6-known-working.dll` and `.pdb` in the native checkout. The built and deployed entry-probe DLL SHA-256 is `D116BC7D9BE3FC51551C6C7D8ADAF105CEA115E526527FD73601A7173D6D0063`; its PDB and both logs are preserved in the same directory. The entry marker was removed after testing; the experimental DLL remains deployed, dormant without a marker. Existing call-site mode can still be selected separately on 1.7.104.

## Read Or Take

The installed MO2 mod is Read Or Take SKSE 1.5.1, DLL version `1.5.1.1.0`, SHA-256 `5037F69E3E9A0A9CB18A19E6C1F3835BC061227604BE5C6B58C2938BA`. Its INI enables automatic tome use. [Published source](https://github.com/powerof3/ReadOrTakeBooks/blob/master/src/Hooks.cpp) installs an activation vfunc replacement and describes pickup → `Read(actor)` → removal on success. It would bypass our old Skyrim call-site patches, but the installed binary was not matched to a source commit or independently disassembled to establish its call target. This run's reported world action reaching the guarded engine entry supports the shared-path hypothesis; it is not a captured DLL call stack. [Lodestone](https://github.com/pprarj/lodestone/blob/main/src/Core/SpellTomes.cpp) uses the same relocation and an inline SafetyHook and warns that a chained hook's apparent original may be another mod's thunk. Its claims do not independently validate our runtime or compatibility.

The user-confirmed world and inventory outcomes are consistent with vanilla behavior in these trials. No separate hook-disabled comparison was run in this task; other SKSE detours, load orders and 1.6.1170 remain unverified. The inventory callback and world callbacks occurred on different threads, so synchronous protection cannot assume checked-main-thread actor or settings access. The first protection decision also needs defensible copy identity or an explicit ambiguity policy to avoid a second punishment. Validate those properties, hook conflict handling and cancellation/consumption semantics before implementing denial.
